Module 1 in one breath: a farm is a simplified, open ecosystem kept alive by pouring in fossil energy, and that same fossil dependence is both losing efficiency and unsettling the very climate the farm depends on.
Read it top to bottom. The dashed line on the right is the feedback loop: the climate disruption farming drives loops back to threaten farming itself.

Start here. Conventional agriculture is built to do one thing, maximize yield, and to do it, it simplifies a diverse ecosystem down to one crop and pours in external inputs.

The catch. A farm is an open ecosystem. Every harvest carries energy and nutrients off the land, so unlike a forest it cannot recycle what it loses. Following the carbon and the energy shows why: energy enters only once, from the sun, and plants catch only about 2% of it, and carbon has just one way in, photosynthesis. Remove the crop and the loop is broken, so the farm survives only by being subsidized, mostly with fossil-fuel energy.

Where it bites. That subsidy is the whole tension. More input has stopped buying more efficiency, yield per unit of energy fell from 1.44 to 0.63, and the same fossil energy, plus the methane from the animals and the soil, is warming the planet. A warmer planet then loops back to threaten the agriculture that helped cause it.

So the arc points one way. Close the loops and cut the subsidy, design farming to work more like the ecosystem it actually is. Every card in the field guide is one piece of that single story.
